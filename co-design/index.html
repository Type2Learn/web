Type2Learn Active Learning · Co-design

Neurodivergent voices should change the product—not decorate it.

Type2Learn has a public early-participation record: one professional, three teachers, five learner contributions, and 25 product reviews. These contributions inform product decisions about accessible flow, sensory controls, clear steps, and support language. The work continues toward structured, safeguarded participation with neurodivergent learners, educators, families, accessibility specialists, and relevant professionals.

Current status: early participation is real and publicly recorded. It is not presented as a completed formal co-design study, clinical validation, endorsement, or a formal advisory relationship. Those claims require their own consent, method, safeguards, and published limitations.

Who the process is intended to involve

People with dyslexia, ADHD, autism, and other learning or access experiences can contribute without being required to disclose a diagnosis publicly. Families and educators can contribute everyday learning context. Relevant professionals can contribute within an explicitly agreed scope.

What contributions have informed—and are intended to influence

Early records have informed the direction for learning structure, accessibility controls, sensory settings, language, feedback, navigation, pacing, alternative input, pause and return. Future structured participation will test those decisions and the ways learners can demonstrate understanding.

How early feedback is recorded—and formal feedback will be collected

The public record links permission-aware source material to a product implication wherever publication is appropriate. Future structured review and accessible task testing require a defined purpose, participant role, age-appropriate consent or assent, safeguarding owner, access needs, information collected, retention, withdrawal, and compensation status.

Public decision status

Early public record: 1 professional, 3 teachers, 5 learner contributions, and 25 product reviews. Being formalised: participation criteria, consent and safeguarding routes, accessible feedback formats, decision records, and contributor recognition. Planned: structured review and task testing before outcome claims are made.

Educational and professional boundaries

General feedback does not create clinical validation, research evidence, endorsement, or a formal advisory role. Type2Learn is educational and does not diagnose, treat, or clinically assess any condition.
