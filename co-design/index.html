Type2Learn Active Learning · Co-design

Neurodivergent voices should change the product—not decorate it.

Type2Learn is being designed to involve neurodivergent learners, educators, families, accessibility specialists, special-education professionals, learning professionals, and learning-science researchers through structured, safeguarded participation.

Current status: the participation model is being prepared. Type2Learn does not yet claim completed external co-design findings, clinical validation, endorsement, or formal advisory relationships.

Who the process is intended to involve

People with dyslexia, ADHD, autism, and other learning or access experiences can contribute without being required to disclose a diagnosis publicly. Families and educators can contribute everyday learning context. Relevant professionals can contribute within an explicitly agreed scope.

What contributors are intended to influence

Learning structure, accessibility controls, sensory settings, language, feedback, navigation, pacing, alternative input, pause and return, and the ways learners can demonstrate understanding.

How feedback is intended to be collected

Planned methods include structured review and accessible task testing. Before participation begins, Type2Learn must define the purpose, participant role, age-appropriate consent or assent, safeguarding owner, access needs, information collected, retention, withdrawal, and whether compensation is offered.

Public decision status

Published findings: none yet. In preparation: participation criteria, consent and safeguarding routes, accessible feedback formats, decision records, and contributor recognition. Planned: structured review and task testing before outcome claims are made.

Educational and professional boundaries

General feedback does not create clinical validation, research evidence, endorsement, or a formal advisory role. Type2Learn is educational and does not diagnose, treat, or clinically assess any condition.
